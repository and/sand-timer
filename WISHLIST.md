# Wishlist

Ideas for later, roughly in the order they'd be worth doing.

## A more real hourglass

- **A real frame.** Wooden or brass discs at the top and bottom joined by three or four turned pillars, in
  front of and behind the glass: Walnut, Oak, Brass, or the black caps of today. Real wood grain and soft
  shine on the pillars.
- **A grainy stream.** The falling thread as separate grains that scatter a little, puffing up where they land
  on the pile, instead of a smooth line.
- **Tilt the phone, the sand leans.** The phone's motion sensor tilts the sand's surface as the phone tilts,
  the way a real one feels in the hand. The renderer already settles sand at any angle.
- **The glass drops to the floor on the phones.** In the clean view, instead of gliding to the middle, the
  glass falls to the bottom of the screen with a small bounce, the sand jolting and the shadow under its base,
  as the Mac's does; a paused one falls onto its side. Picking the phone up lifts it back. A setting: the
  glass rests in the middle or on the bottom, just above the home gesture.
- **True 3D glass.** SceneKit or Metal, with real refraction and the sand as thousands of grains. The most
  real of all, but a rewrite of the renderer and heavier on the battery.

## More places

- **Linux (and Windows).** Compose for Desktop, reusing the Android app's glass, timer, record, projects and
  statistics, in a borderless, see-through, always-on-top window with a tray menu and the chime, packaged as
  .deb, .rpm or AppImage. Linking later: Bluetooth on Linux means BlueZ over D-Bus.

Done in the meantime: grains of several tones with a glint here and there, the top of each heap lit, the sand
shaded where it presses on the glass, the walls darker through thicker glass, a window's reflection, light
gathered onto the base, the shadow on the phones, and a faint sand-coloured glow beside it.
